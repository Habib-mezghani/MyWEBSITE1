Building Energy Simulation Software

May 27, 2018

Under an Energy Performance Contract (EPC), the energy consumption of a building must be estimated reliably over its whole life cycle. Delivering an EPC therefore requires effective tools, and the most essential of these is energy simulation software (see this directory for a full list of available tools). Such software models the energy needs of a building and estimates its consumption.

For an existing building, it can also be used to build renovation scenarios, compare energy-saving measures, and set up a guarantee on the actual performance.

Below is a summary of the strengths of three tools that are widely used by engineers in France, and that I have used myself during my studies and my professional work.

  • ClimaWin is a multi-purpose program that combines, from a single set of inputs, heat loss and cooling load calculations using the ASHRAE method, French thermal regulation calculations (RT 2012), and energy consumption calculations. Loads are calculated with the Radiant Time Series (RTS) method, and the regulatory calculation follows the Th-BCE 2012 rules (the algorithms developed by the CSTB for RT 2012), as implemented by BBS Slama. Compared with the regulatory calculation, the energy module accepts more detailed and flexible inputs, such as the real site and weather file, occupancy schedules, equipment and lighting.

  • DesignBuilder has been built from the start around BIM concepts (with gbXML import) and an inheritance approach to model inputs: every room created within a block automatically inherits the settings of its parent block. This improves productivity, avoids entering the same information several times, and lets the model evolve smoothly through each project phase while keeping the interface easy to use. In my view, one of its main strengths is that it runs on EnergyPlus, the simulation engine developed by the US Department of Energy (DOE). EnergyPlus simulates the building, its HVAC systems, and its natural and artificial lighting together in a single coupled calculation.

  • Pléiades (Comfie engine), developed by Izuba Énergies, is used to design buildings and assess their energy and environmental performance. Its graphical modeller makes it quick to enter the building envelope, thermal properties, shading, systems and usage scenarios. The thermal calculation uses the Comfie engine, developed by the Energy Efficiency Systems Centre of École des Mines de Paris, and daylighting is calculated with Radiance. Equipment sizing follows internationally recognised methods: the EN 12831 standard for heating, and the ASHRAE Radiant Time Series (RTS) method for cooling. In 2016, Kocliko, a start-up founded by two researchers from École des Mines de Paris, released a tool for Pléiades models that automates batches of simulations, making sensitivity analysis, uncertainty analysis and cost optimisation much easier.
